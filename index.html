Where it started
UX Design & Research

Every product decision I make starts with how real people actually use the product, not just in design. Since my teens I’ve instinctively designed interfaces to get users to their goal fast, not just to look good, with a little visual bliss on top. It started with logos and web interfaces for esports teams and early Flash interactions, moved through browser-game UIs and ended in HCD-led web and mobile app product design, always built on real behavioral data and aimed at a business metric. Research came first, the market and the person using it. Design was the foundation, growth and product became the career, and the principle never changed.

Code & Builder

I speak engineering’s language from code to agile delivery, so product and growth decisions don’t get lost in translation. Design was a choice, code an unwanted trial by fire. Three years of object-oriented C++ at school led to a .NET Win XP tune-up tool coded by two friends and me, and to tutoring informatics master’s students while I was still on my own non-informatics curriculum. During the blogging era I built, grew, monetised and sold blogs, custom-made WordPress themes and widgets, running these first ventures on Kanban boards, with Scrum and Extreme Programming following over the years. I’ve never worked as an engineer, but I can read and understand a codebase, build product and growth experiments, and work eye to eye with engineering.

Founder

I’ve built ventures end to end, and learned most from what didn’t work. It started at 15 with web design services that grew into a two-person agency collaborating with a network of external partners, followed by a travel-experience platform. The one that taught me early on was born from a pain I felt first-hand as a student, a feedback aggregator for universities and academies. The users felt the value, but it was so new that procurement had no category for it, and every approval meant dozens of policy loops in already slow-moving universities. One of my first lessons in letting the market steer, and in how institutions actually decide. When a large company signed on as a paying pilot customer after a first conversation at an event, the venture pivoted to B2B. These are just a few of the experiences that led to methods like the "Pain-Gain Balance" for value proposition optimization and positioning, which form the intrapreneurial product innovation system in my book, Known Before Built. My journey was anything but straight, with plenty of failures, plenty of small wins, and two exits, stories better told in person.

The operator's side
Growth, product & marketing

I run innovation, growth marketing and product as one revenue system, and I’ve built every channel in it myself. My growth path began before Panda with technical SEO, experimenting with early black-hat methods and exploiting algorithm gaps to engineer organic search visibility at scale. One of those projects, scaled to over 100,000 monthly unique visitors in as early as 2010, before I sold it. That quantitative foundation evolved into full-funnel conversion rate optimisation, combining data analysis with my design background, and into organic social and content. As the teams and products I worked with matured toward scale, I expanded into paid acquisition, building performance marketing across paid social channels (pioneering the granular behavioral segmentation we all miss from power-seller) and search. Together, these form the core of my T-shaped profile, anchored by continuous product discovery, validation and rapid experimentation.

Principles What I keep repeating.

Don’t ask for opinions. Ask for behaviour.

"Opinions describe what people believe they would do. Behaviour shows what they did. Reported behaviour beats opinion, and observed behaviour beats both."
— from my book Known Before Built

They are the rock stars. You are their fan.

Customers, buyers and users are the experts on their own problems. We need to listen and observe to understand their reality to see what they can't see.

Research is the foundation of everything.

Know the market, the customers, the buyers and the users in depth. That is how you find the right product and also the channels to grow it.

Good ideas need safety to surface.

Teams often lack the safety to say the unpopular thing, regardless of hierarchy. Small units of five to seven, scaled through unit leads rather than bigger teams. That’s how I lead, and how I’d grow a department. In my experience, that’s how compliance theatre gets quieter and productivity goes up. I call these business-changing ideas that never surface "Trapped Capital". It’s also Chapter 1 of Known Before Built.

Hire for understanding, not recitation.

Processes can be taught. Skills can be honed. Intellectual honesty can’t. I look for people who understand a method well enough to know when not to use it.

Shared wins teach a little. Shared failures teach a lot.

Design, marketing, sales, growth, engineering and customer success learn faster when they’re aligned, and when the failures travel as freely as the successes. Most problems are system problems, not people problems, and the system needs to celebrate every lesson learned to be truly aligned. Alignment often doesn't follow the org chart. It follows the people others turn to for guidance, whatever their title. I call winning them over "Earning the right to operate", a section in Chapter 8 of Known Before Built.
